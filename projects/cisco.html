Overview

I worked on Cisco Meraki Fabric, a new product area within the Meraki ecosystem, helping shape early workflows, interface patterns, and dashboard experiences for complex enterprise infrastructure.

The work involved frequent iteration and cross-team collaboration to make sure new Fabric concepts aligned with existing Meraki products, design patterns, and system logic instead of feeling like a separate tool.

Case study in progress

This page focuses on selected Cisco Meraki work across new Fabric product design, cross-team alignment, enterprise dashboard patterns, and legacy interface cleanup.

The challenge

Cisco Meraki Fabric was a new product area inside an established enterprise platform.

The challenge was not simply to design a new experience from scratch. It was to shape a new product direction while making sure every pattern, workflow, and interaction could fit within the broader Meraki ecosystem.

The work required ongoing iteration, cross-functional review, and close alignment with other teams so Fabric could support new infrastructure workflows without feeling disconnected from the rest of the product system.

What I designed

I worked across selected Cisco Meraki Fabric design efforts, including new product area exploration, dashboard layout patterns, infrastructure status views, table and data-heavy workflows, information hierarchy, navigation structure, design system alignment, and interaction details for enterprise workflows.

I also supported cleanup of legacy interface areas where needed, helping bring more clarity and consistency to the broader product experience.

The main design challenge was creating something new for Fabric while making sure it still felt native to Meraki's existing product ecosystem.

Design decisions

New, but not separate

Fabric needed its own product direction while still feeling native to the broader Meraki system.

Alignment before polish

A large part of the work was making sure the experience worked across teams, product areas, and system constraints before pushing visual polish.

Hierarchy first

Dense dashboards need clear visual priority so users can quickly understand what matters.

Design for scanning

IT teams often need to move fast, so layouts, cards, labels, and status states had to support quick reading.

Consistency across complex screens

Reusable patterns helped make different areas feel part of the same product system.

Clarity without oversimplifying

The goal was to reduce friction while preserving the technical depth required by enterprise users.

What this project shows

Cisco Meraki shows how I approach enterprise product design inside a mature product ecosystem: shaping new product areas, working through cross-team alignment, and creating clearer experiences for technical users.

It also reflects my ability to design within real product constraints, where the value is not only in the final UI, but in the iteration, system thinking, alignment, and decisions that help a new product area fit into a larger platform.
