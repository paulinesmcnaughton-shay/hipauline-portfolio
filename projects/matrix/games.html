The featured game

Little Sort is a cozy casual puzzle game built around sorting, matching, and satisfying completion moments.

The game started as a simple sorting concept and evolved into a multi-mode puzzle experience with 500 Match It levels, 100 Tubes levels, daily play, sound effects, haptics, power-ups, progression, and monetization scaffolding. I designed and built the experience end to end, including the visual system, gameplay flows, level selection, onboarding, reward loops, settings, ads, in-app purchase scaffolding, and launch checklist.

The design challenge

Make the game feel simple on the surface while supporting enough depth to stay interesting over time.

For Little Sort and Tubes, the rules needed to be immediately understandable. Players should know what they can move, where they can move it, and why a move is valid, without a long explanation.

For Match It, the challenge was different. The game needed enough variety to support hundreds of levels without feeling random, unfair, or repetitive.

Key design decisions

1

Make the rules visible through the UI

In the original Tubes mode, items sat on shelves. The logic worked, but the shelf metaphor made the rules harder to read. Players were sorting stacked items while the layout looked like shelf placement.

I changed the presentation to vertical glass tubes so the rule became visually obvious: pick up the top item, pour it into a matching or empty tube. A presentation change, not a logic rewrite. The existing rules stayed intact while the UI now communicates how the game works.

2

Keep the core game screen consistent

Little Sort, Tubes, and Match It each have different mechanics, but they share consistent patterns for header controls, settings, info, sound, haptics, and level navigation, so the game feels like one product instead of separate mini-games stitched together.

3

Add variety without breaking the core loop

Match It layers in several mechanics over time, each added carefully so it expands the game without making the early experience feel overwhelming.

  • Move-based levels
  • Timed levels
  • Color collection goals
  • Breakable blockers
  • Rainbow diamond tiles
  • Hard & super-hard spikes
  • Power-ups
  • Rewarded-ad continues
  • Daily member gifts
4

Design for launch, not just a prototype

I treated the game like a real product from the start, with settings, restore purchases, account deletion flows, ads and IAP scaffolding, test IDs, privacy and terms placeholders, level testing, and store-readiness tasks.

The result is closer to a launch-ready mobile product than a design mockup.

Current status

Near launch

Core gameplay systems are built and level-tested for fairness and clarity. Match It is the most complete mode, with 500 levels and multiple mechanics live.

The final gameplay task is updating Tubes mode from a shelf-style layout to a true vertical glass-tube UI. After that, the remaining work is mostly launch plumbing: real ads, real in-app purchases, auth, App Store assets, privacy links, and production build testing.

What I learned

This project pushed me beyond traditional product design into full product building. I had to think through not only screens and flows, but game logic, difficulty pacing, edge cases, player motivation, monetization, testing, and launch requirements.

It also sharpened my AI-assisted product workflow. I used AI tools to move faster, but the product decisions, UX judgment, visual direction, testing, and prioritization came from me.

The biggest lesson: a game only feels simple when the system behind it is carefully designed.
